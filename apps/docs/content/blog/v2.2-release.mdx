## The "One Note Per Paper" Dilemma

If you have ever set up an academic workflow in Obsidian, you are likely familiar with the standard advice: _create a separate Markdown file for every paper in your Zotero library._

In practice, this advice leads directly to "literature note fatigue":

- **Hollow vaults:** Importing 80 papers from a literature search produces 80 nearly empty notes whose only purpose is to hold frontmatter properties like `status: to-read` or `priority: high`.
- **Duplicate storage:** Copying multi-megabyte PDFs into your vault bloats sync limits and backup sizes.
- **Fragmented reading:** You read and highlight in Zotero, switch to Obsidian to paste text and format citekeys, then switch back to Zotero when you need context.
- **Disconnected graphs:** Unless you manually cross-link every entry with wikilinks, literature notes float in the graph view as isolated islands, severed from the academic discourse they represent.

ZotLit 2.2 changes this paradigm. It treats your local Zotero SQLite database as your permanent literature repository, and Obsidian as your active thinking canvas. You only create a literature note when you genuinely have your own thoughts to write.

## 1. Browse and Inspect Without Context-Switching: The Library View

Constantly switching between Zotero and Obsidian breaks deep focus. The new **Library View** brings a full-featured Zotero browser into your Obsidian sidebar or main workspace.

![The Library View with My Library filtered to papers with PDFs, and the Reading Card of one paper showing its tags, its PDF, and its annotation counts by color](/img/blog/v2.2-library-view.webp)

### Explore Collections, Tags, and Active Research

- **Library tree:** Navigate your libraries, collections, subcollections, and tags with real-time paper counts.
- **Quick facets:** Filter instantly to unread papers, annotated items, papers with literature notes, or papers with PDF attachments.
- **The Reading Card:** Select any paper to expand its Reading Card. Read the full abstract, check bibliographic metadata, and review annotation color breakdowns without opening external windows.
- **Drag to cite:** Drag any paper row directly into an open editor tab to insert a formatted citation or a literature note link.
- **Smart "Recent" queue:** The Recent list ranks papers based on your actual research activity—opening notes, reading PDFs, citing works, or creating annotations.

### "Show in Library" Everywhere

Whenever you encounter a citation in Obsidian—whether in the References sidebar, hover popovers, graph nodes, or backlink panels—a single click on **Show in Library** reveals that paper in the Library View with its search cleared and row highlighted.

![A thesis chapter with the citation popover open over a Rougier et al. citation and the tooltip "Show in Zotero library" on its library button, beside the Library View with that paper's row selected and its details below the list](/img/blog/v2.2-show-in-library.webp)

## 2. In-Vault PDF Reading Backed by Zotero: Zero Bloat, Two-Way Sync

Reading academic PDFs in Obsidian used to require copying large PDF files into your vault folder. ZotLit 2.2 changes this completely.

![A paper open in Obsidian's PDF reader with its Zotero highlights and underlines, one highlight's popup showing its comment and tag, and the annotation view listing each annotation as a card](/img/blog/v2.2-pdf-reader-annotations.webp)

### Zero Vault Storage Overhead

With Obsidian 1.14.2+, ZotLit opens PDFs directly from your external Zotero storage folder inside an Obsidian editor tab. Your vault stores zero duplicate PDF files, saving gigabytes of disk space and sync bandwidth.

### Real-Time Two-Way Annotation Sync (Zotero 10+)

When connected to Zotero 10, annotations are fully bidirectional:

- **Full toolset:** Highlight, underline, add sticky notes, type on-page text, capture figures or tables with rectangular image selections, and draw freehand ink strokes directly inside Obsidian.
- **Live database persistence:** Every mark you make in Obsidian writes straight back to Zotero's database. When you open the same PDF in Zotero, your annotations, comments, and color codes are already there.
- **Instant Obsidian reflection:** Highlights created in Zotero appear immediately in Obsidian's reader and annotation sidebar.

![A thesis chapter note with an inserted annotation callout that links back to page 1 of the PDF, beside the PDF and the annotation view with the same annotation selected](/img/blog/v2.2-pdf-reader-insert-into-note.webp)

### Rich Annotation Popovers & Universal Links

## 3. Manage Literature Without Note Bloat: ZotLit Query and the Query View

You should not need a Markdown file just to track whether you have read a paper. ZotLit 2.2 introduces standalone **Query Files (`.zotquery`)** and embedded **Query Blocks** that render directly over your local Zotero library.

![A table of papers with reading status, note, related, done, rating, and finished columns from each literature note, with the status cell of one paper open for editing and suggesting read, reading, to-read, and unread](/img/blog/v2.2-reading-status-table.webp)

### In-Cell Property Editing (Like Obsidian Bases)

Switch any query file to the **Table** layout and add literature note frontmatter properties as columns—such as `reading-status`, `priority`, `rating`, or custom tags:

- **Interactive cells:** Click a cell to toggle a checkbox, pick from tag chips, select a date, or choose a status option.
- **Notes on demand:** A paper without a note displays `(no note)`. You can read, filter, and track it as long as you like. When you finally edit a property cell or decide to write a summary, ZotLit generates the note on demand.
- **Native metadata alignment:** Properties live in your note's frontmatter, meaning Obsidian's native Properties panel, Dataview, and Obsidian Bases always stay in sync.

![A query file showing papers in a table with reading status and note columns, beside a note whose embedded query block groups annotations by paper](/img/blog/v2.2-query-file-and-block.webp)

### Embedded Query Blocks

Inside your notes, you can embed query blocks that display items or annotations dynamically. The default literature note template now embeds an interactive query block for annotations instead of copying static text. When you add or adjust highlights in Zotero or Obsidian, they appear immediately in your note without running manual note updates.

![A literature note with an embedded query block that groups the paper's annotations by color, with the Yellow group's four cards in two columns above the Red group](/img/blog/v2.2-query-block-in-note.webp)

## 4. The Citation Graph as a Living Research Map

In academic writing, citations represent the intellectual web that connects ideas. In ZotLit 2.2, citations become native citizens of Obsidian's graph view.

![The global graph view with purple citation edges linking literature notes such as Bush 1945, Nelson 1965, and Luhmann 1981, each labelled with its author, year, and title, three research notes, and the dimmed Cited Work Node Engelbart 1962](/img/blog/v2.2-graph-view-citations.webp)

### Automatic Citation Edges & Dimmed Work Nodes

- **Connected literature:** Notes automatically draw citation edges to the papers they cite. When two notes reference the same study, they converge on that paper's node.
- **Cited Work Nodes:** If you cite a paper that does not yet have a literature note, it appears as a distinct, dimmed Cited Work Node. Right-click the node to generate its literature note, copy its citation key, or view it in Zotero.
- **Author-title labels & popovers:** Display author names, publication years, and short titles directly over nodes in the graph. Hovering over a node displays its formatted bibliographic entry.
- **Instant research map commands:** Run **Open citation graph** or **Open local citation graph** to isolate literature connections, highlight citation trajectories, and hide unrelated vault clutter.

## 5. Unified Profile Documents & The Template Workbench

In ZotLit v2.1, customizing templates meant juggling separate files for notes, managed regions, filenames, and frontmatter. ZotLit 2.2 consolidates all of this into a unified, visual architecture.

![The Template Workbench behind the Literature note profiles settings page, which lists the Default profile, a Books profile that applies automatically, and the book-details partial](/img/blog/v2.2-literature-note-profiles.webp)

### Single-Document Profiles & Automatic Matching

- **Unified profile documents:** Each profile is now a single Markdown file (`zotlit-profile.<slug>.md`) containing the note name pattern, destination folder, frontmatter rules, body template, and annotation layout.
- **Profile matching:** Define match criteria based on item types (e.g., books vs. journal articles), Zotero collections, or tags. ZotLit applies the appropriate profile automatically when creating notes.
- **Your notes belong to you:** The default template no longer wraps your notes in rigid `%%zt-managed%%` markers. The initial note body is generated once; after that, it is entirely yours to write and reorganize.

![The Template Workbench layout for The Semantic Web: the Fields pane on the left, the Default profile in the middle with its shared-template notice and the template text, and the Note preview on the right](/img/blog/v2.2-template-workbench-layout.webp)

### The Interactive Template Workbench

The **Template Workbench** provides a dedicated three-column workspace for authoring templates:

1. **Fields pane:** Browse all typed Zotero metadata available for the selected item.
2. **Editor:** Author Liquid template rules with real-time field auto-completion.
3. **Live preview:** Inspect the rendered note output against an actual paper from your library before saving changes.

## 6. Performance, Search, and Power Tools

ZotLit 2.2 includes deep engineering improvements under the hood:

- **Asynchronous database engine:** Database queries, citation indexing, and full-text lookups run in background threads. Typing in search modals and citation pickers remains fluid, even in libraries with tens of thousands of items.
- **Chinese word segmentation:** An optional segmenter splits Chinese titles into searchable words, making literature search across non-Latin scripts fast and accurate.
- **Query CLI:** Query your Zotero library from the Obsidian CLI (`zotlit:query`) to export structured JSON for custom research scripts, data analysis, or LLM agents.

## Get ZotLit 2.2

1. In Obsidian, open **Settings > Community plugins**, select **Browse**, and install or update **ZotLit**.
2. In Zotero, install the matching [ZotLit Companion](/docs/install-companion) add-on.
3. Open the command palette and run **ZotLit: Open Library View** or **ZotLit: New Query File**.
