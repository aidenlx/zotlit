This tutorial walks you from your Zotero library to your first literature note. You will open the library in Obsidian, read a paper's PDF with your Zotero highlights on the page, and then write a note for the paper beside it. By the end, you will have a Markdown note with the paper's title, a link back to Zotero, and the annotations you made on the PDF.

<Callout type="info" title="Before you start">
  [Install ZotLit](/docs/install-zotlit). ZotLit finds your Zotero database
  automatically from your Zotero profile, and Zotero does not need to be open.
</Callout>

## Open your library

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Open the Library View

    On a new install, ZotLit opens the Library View for you, in a tab titled <UiLabel name={m.library_view_name()} />. Otherwise, select the ZotLit icon in the ribbon, or open the command palette (`Ctrl+P` on Windows/Linux, `Cmd+P` on macOS) and run:

    <Command name={m.command_open_library_view_name()} />

    A setup banner may sit above the list. You can leave it there for now: once its <UiLabel name={m.welcome_step_connect_title()} /> step is done, ZotLit is reading your library.

    If the view shows <UiLabel name={m.library_view_not_found_title()} /> instead of your papers, select <UiLabel name={m.library_view_locate()} /> and choose your Zotero data folder.

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Find a paper you have highlighted

    For this tutorial, choose a paper that already has annotations, the highlights and notes you made in the Zotero PDF reader. That way you can see them in Obsidian.

    Under <UiLabel name={m.library_view_facets_label()} /> on the left, select <UiLabel name={m.library_view_facet_annotated()} />. The list now shows only the papers you have annotated. To look for one paper, type part of its title in the <UiLabel name={m.library_view_search_label()} /> box.

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### Select the paper

    Click the paper's row. The Reading Card opens on the right of the view, with the paper's abstract and its annotations. If you do not see it, widen the pane: the card needs room beside the list.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Read the paper

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Open the PDF beside the library

    Right-click the paper's row and choose <UiLabel name={m.library_view_menu_open_pdf_right()} />.

    The PDF opens in a new pane to the right of the library. ZotLit does not copy the file into your vault.

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### See your highlights

    The highlights and notes you made in Zotero are already on the page, in the same colors. The <UiLabel name={m.annot_view_name()} /> view also opens in the right sidebar and lists each annotation as a card.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## Write the note

<Steps>
  <Step>
    ### Write the note in a new tab

    Click the paper's row in the library again. Then press `Ctrl+Enter` (`Cmd+Enter` on macOS).

    ZotLit writes the literature note and opens it in a new tab in front of the library. The PDF stays in its pane on the right, so you can read on one side and write on the other. You will see a brief notice: <UiLabel name={m.notice_created_note()} />

    Plain `Enter` writes the same note, but opens it in the pane beside the library, in place of the PDF.

  </Step>
  <Step>
    ### See the result

    The note appears in a `literatures` folder in your vault (the default location). It contains:

    - A heading with the paper's title
    - A link back to the item in Zotero
    - Links to its attached files
    - An **Annotations** section with your PDF highlights and notes

    The note's filename is the item's citation key by default.

    ZotLit built this note from the Default profile, the built-in one that decides a note's name, folder, properties, and body. Read about [profiles](/docs/concepts/profiles) to see what you can change.

  </Step>
</Steps>

## What you are looking at

The note contains a marked section called the **managed region** (between `%%zt-managed%%` and `%%/zt-managed%%` markers). ZotLit owns this region; your own writing goes above or below it. When you update the note later, ZotLit refreshes the managed region while leaving your writing intact.

For more on how this works, see [Literature notes and the managed region](/docs/concepts/literature-notes-and-the-managed-region).

## Opening an existing note

Back in the library, the paper's row now shows that it has a literature note. Select the row and press `Enter` again, and ZotLit opens the existing note instead of creating a duplicate.

You can also reach any paper's note without the library: run <Command inline name={m.command_open_lit_note_name()} /> from the command palette and search for the paper.
