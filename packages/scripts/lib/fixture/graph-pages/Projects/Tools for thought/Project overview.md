# Tools for thought: project overview

This project traces one question through seventy years of writing: what should a machine do for a person who reads and writes for a living?

## The augmentation line

Bush starts from the scientist who cannot keep up with the record. His memex lets a reader join two documents into a trail and follow it again later [@bushAsWeMayThink1945]. Licklider moves the question from storage to partnership: a computer that works with the person in real time, during the problem rather than after it [@lickliderManComputerSymbiosis1960].

Engelbart turns both ideas into a research programme for augmenting the intellect of a whole team [@engelbartAugmentingHuman1962]. Kay and Goldberg bring the same goal to a personal machine that anyone can learn [@kayPersonalDynamicMedia1977]. Nelson, writing for a general public, argues that this kind of computing belongs to everyone [@nelsonComputerLib1974].

## Where the tools fell short

The NoteCards team listed seven problems that hypermedia systems still had to solve, from search to versioning [@halaszReflectionsNoteCards1988]. Matuschak and Nielsen make a similar point thirty years later: most note-taking software copies the shape of these early systems without their ambition [@matuschakTransformativeTools2019].

Luhmann's paper slip box is a useful counterexample, because it works without any software at all [@luhmannCommunicatingSlipBoxes1981].
