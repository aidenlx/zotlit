# Memory and learning

Notes on what experimental psychology says about remembering what we read.

## Forgetting and retrieval

Ebbinghaus measured his own forgetting of nonsense syllables and described how quickly newly learned material is lost [@ebbinghausGedachtnis1885]. Roediger and Karpicke show that a test is more than a measure: students who were tested on a passage remembered more of it later than students who studied it again [@roedigerTestEnhancedLearning2006, p. 249].

Matuschak and Nielsen build this into a reading format, with review prompts inside the essay itself [@matuschakTransformativeTools2019].

## Load and effort

Working memory is small. Sweller shows that some problem-solving methods use so much of it that little is left for learning [@swellerCognitiveLoad1988]. The longhand note-taking result may be the other side of this: the effort of summarizing in one's own words is where the learning happens [@muellerPenMightier2014].

## What this means for my notes

- Write each literature note in my own words, not as a list of quotations.
- Review old notes by trying to recall them first, then reading.
- Writing things down frees memory for other work [@riskoCognitiveOffloading2016], but it can also stop me from learning them. Forte's capture habit has the same risk [@forteBuildingSecondBrain2022].
