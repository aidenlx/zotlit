## The shape of an answer page

An answer page reader is comparing or stuck: they want to know which way to
go, whether something is safe, or why something happened. They are not in
the middle of a step. Where the sections above speak of the lesson or the
course, they describe the same reader; an answer page has the pictures the
fact sheet lists.

- **Answer:** two or three sentences that answer the page's question, so the
  first screen is enough for a reader who reads nothing else.
- **Table:** one table, one row per option or case the fact sheet lists, with
  the columns the fact sheet names. The table carries the comparison; the
  words around it say only what the table cannot.
- **Sections:** the fact sheet's sections, in its order, each a heading and a
  short paragraph or a short list. Where a section tells the reader to do
  something, link to the recipe the fact sheet names.
- **Questions:** three to six questions in the reader's own words, each with
  an answer of one to three sentences.

The page is 600 to 900 words.

The title is one plain statement of the question or task, at most 60
characters, in the words a researcher types into a search engine; the fact
sheet lists those words. Name Zotero or Obsidian when the question involves
them. The sidebar label is two to four words. The description is one
sentence, at most 160 characters, with the answer in the first 120.

The facts come from the fact sheet alone: every label, every option, every
answer. Where the fact sheet is silent, leave it out.

## What to return

Markdown, in this order: `Title`, `Sidebar label`, `Description`, then
`## Answer`, `## Table`, each section under its own `##` heading,
`## Questions` with each question as a bold line followed by its answer, and
`Figure request` where you ask for one, under the section it belongs to.
