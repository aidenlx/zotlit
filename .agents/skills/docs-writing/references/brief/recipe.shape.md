## The shape of a recipe

A recipe reader is in the middle of a task. They searched for one thing,
they have Obsidian open, and they will leave the page as soon as they have
the answer. Where the sections above speak of the lesson or the course, they
describe the same reader; on a recipe, each step has the screenshot the fact
sheet lists in place of a recording.

- **Answer:** one or two sentences that say what to do, so the first screen
  answers the search on its own.
- **Steps:** numbered, one action each, naming the control by its on-screen
  label, then the one thing the reader sees when it worked.
- **Fixes:** each fix the fact sheet lists, as the thing the reader sees
  followed by what to do.
- **Related:** the links the fact sheet lists, each with one line on when
  the reader needs it.

The answer and the steps together stay near 300 words. The fixes and the
related links sit below them in fold-outs, which the reader opens only when
they need one. A long list of settings or commands belongs on the reference
page the fact sheet names; link to it. Where a step needs a reason, give it in
one or two sentences at that step.

The title is one plain statement of the task, at most 60 characters, in the
words a researcher types into a search engine; the fact sheet lists those
words. Name Zotero or Obsidian when the task involves them. The sidebar label
is two to four words. The description is one sentence, at most 160
characters, with the answer in the first 120.

The facts come from the fact sheet alone: every label, every result, every
fix, every example the reader types. Where the fact sheet is silent, leave
it out.

## What to return

Markdown, in this order: `Title`, `Sidebar label`, `Description`, then
`## Answer`, `## Steps` as a numbered list (each item the action, then the
check on its own line), `## Fixes` with each fix as a bold line of what the
reader sees followed by what to do, `## Related`, and `Figure request` where
you ask for one, under the step it belongs to.
